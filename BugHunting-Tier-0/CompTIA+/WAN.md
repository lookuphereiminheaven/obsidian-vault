- Types of WAN technologies
  1. Public Switched Telephone Network - PSTN
    - Most common physical infrastructure used in WAN due to its widespread ability
    1. Dial-up
         - Uses PSTN to transmit traffic as an analog signal
         - Requires analog modem to format network traffic
         - Maximum speed 56 Kbps
    2. Integrated Services Digital Network - ISDN
       - Digital P2P WAN using PSTN
       - Completely digital
       - Requires use of terminal adapter (TA) for connection to end node (digital modem)
       - Primary Rate of Interface (PRI) uses 23 64 Kbps B channels and 1 64Kbps D channel for call setup and link management
         - Achieves speed of 1.544 Mbps
       - Commonly implemented as a Basic Rate Interface (BRI) using 2 B channels and 1 D channel
         - Achieves speed of 128 Kbps speed
      - Not as capable of Digital Subscriber Line (DSL) but used where DSL can't be used
    3. xDSL
       - A digital WAN using PSTN
       - requires digital modem
       - Dedicated digital line between end point and a class 5 central office (CO)
       - It is only possible within 18,000 feet of CO
       - carries voice and data
    4. Symmetric DSL - SDSL
       - Synchronous (same upload and download speed)
       - No voice
    5. Asymmetric DSL - ADSL
       - Upload speed slower than download speed
       - Carries data and voice
       - Upload speed of 768 Kbps with download speed of up to 9 Mbps
    6. Very high bit rate DSL - VDSL
       - Like ADSL but used for high quality video and VoIP
       - Limited to download speed of 52 Mbps and upload of 12 Mbps
       - Only possible to use within 4,000 feet of a CO 
       - Allows for up to 100 Mbps over PSTN
       - End point must be within 300 M of CO
  2. Broadband cable
  3. Fiber optic
  4. GSM
  5. CDMA
  6. Point to Point Protocol - PPP
     - Common data link layer used with leased line network
     - Transmits multiple layer 3 protocols through control protocols
     - Allows for multiple physical interfaces to be bonded together and act as a single logical interface, increasing available bandwidth
